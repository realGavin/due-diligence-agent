## Thesis

Chipotle's single-segment, company-owned restaurant model (3,938 U.S. and 104 international units, plus 14 partner-operated) concentrates all economics in one brand and format, and recent data shows that concentration offers no internal diversification against a stalling same-store engine: comparable sales fell 1.7% in FY2025 on a 2.9% transaction decline, operating income was flat at $1.9B and net income flat at $1.5B despite revenue rising to $11.9B, and management guides to roughly flat comps in 2026 — meaning the growth narrative implicit in the original thesis must be tempered by evidence that top-line expansion is no longer converting into profit growth, even as the stock trades at multiples (31.18x P/E, 33.08x P/FCF) that appear to assume continued acceleration. <sub>[S2] [S36] [F40] [F7] [F8] [F11] [F12] [F4] [F47] [F48]</sub>

**PM's rationale:** The core same-store sales engine has turned negative (comps -1.7% with a 2.9% transaction decline) and management guides to flat comps in 2026, while operating income and net income have both stalled at $1.9B and $1.5B despite revenue growth, and EPS gains are mostly mechanical from buybacks that are now visibly straining the cash balance ($350.5M, down from $748.5M). These are high-severity, filing-confirmed issues that undercut the growth narrative, yet the balance sheet remains debt-free, FCF generation is still solid ($1.4B), and digital/Chipotlane investment continues to scale — so this is not yet a clear pass, but the deceleration and margin stagnation at premium multiples (31.18x P/E) warrant close monitoring rather than active pursuit. <sub>[S36] [F7] [F8] [F11] [F12] [F24] [F23] [F42] [F47]</sub>
