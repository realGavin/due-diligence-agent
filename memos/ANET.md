## Thesis

Arista's differentiated, open-standards network-as-a-service platform is capturing outsized AI/cloud switching demand, delivering 28.6% YoY revenue growth to $9.0B with 64.1% gross margin, 42.8% operating margin, and 28.4% ROE, funded by only $119.5M of capex and generating $4.3B of free cash flow (47.2% FCF margin). This is a real, high-return growth franchise, but the margin and growth durability embedded in the thesis are more fragile than a simple extrapolation suggests: gross margin is already flat YoY and structurally exposed to large-customer discounting and supply cost inflation, growth is concentrated in two customers whose demand management itself calls unpredictable, and the entire product line sits on a single sole-source silicon vendor. Position sizing should reflect that this is a quality compounder with identifiable, customer- and supplier-concentration-driven volatility risk, not a smooth, linearly extrapolable growth story. <sub>[F46] [F4] [F41] [F42] [F45] [F28] [F48] [F49] [S28] [S24] [S41]</sub>

**PM's rationale:** The underlying financial profile is exceptional — 28.6% revenue growth, 64.1% gross margin, 47.2% FCF margin, and 28.4% ROE — but the red team surfaced high-severity, well-documented structural risks (sole-source Broadcom dependence, two-customer concentration at 26% and 16% of revenue, management's own admission that near-term revenue trends 'may not be reflective of current demand levels,' and flat gross margin amid large-customer discounting and memory cost pressure) that are not yet resolved by available evidence. These are monitorable via public disclosures (customer concentration trends, gross margin commentary, capex guidance) rather than requiring an immediate pass, but they are material enough that a 'dig deeper' full underwrite is warranted only once the diligence questions on customer durability and supplier terms are answered. <sub>[F46] [F41] [F49] [F45] [S28] [S24] [S41] [S48]</sub>

## Bull case

- Profitability is hardware-atypical: 64.1% gross margin, 42.8% operating margin, and 39.0% net margin in FY2025, with operating income growing from $1.5B in FY2022 to $3.9B in FY2025, reflecting a largely software/systems-driven value proposition. <sub>[F41] [F42] [F43] [F9] [F12]</sub>
- The model is capital-light and highly cash generative: FY2025 operating cash flow of $4.4B and free cash flow of $4.3B (47.2% FCF margin) were produced against only $119.5M of capex, supporting a 28.4% ROE on a growing $12.4B equity base. <sub>[F24] [F48] [F49] [F28] [F45] [F36]</sub>
- Balance sheet strength and minimal dilution reinforce quality: stockholders' equity grew from $4.9B in FY2022 to $12.4B in FY2025, while diluted share count was essentially flat at 1.28B (-0.4% YoY). <sub>[F33] [F36] [F40] [F39] [F50]</sub>
- Continued R&D investment ($728.4M in FY2022 to $1.2B in FY2025, 13.7% of revenue) funds an expanding Etherlink portfolio of over twenty AI networking products and diversification into Campus/SD-WAN and Cloud-Grade Routing, broadening the addressable market beyond hyperscalers. <sub>[F17] [F20] [F44] [S9] [S11] [S12]</sub>

## Bear case (after red-team review)

- Extreme customer concentration is the largest company-specific risk: two end customers represented 26% and 16% of FY2025 revenue, with the smaller of the two swinging from 21% in 2023 to 15% in 2024 to 16% in 2025, meaning loss or delay of orders from either could materially move reported revenue. <sub>[S28] [S39]</sub>
- Arista is critically dependent on a single sole-source merchant silicon vendor, Broadcom; failure to innovate, product delays, supply shortages, or unfavorable terms could make products less competitive, delay launches, or force redesigns, directly threatening the revenue and gross-margin base the thesis relies on. <sub>[S24] [S27] [F41] [F4]</sub>
- Gross margin durability is questionable: management explicitly states large-customer deals carry pricing discounts, bundled upgrades, and extended terms that reduce gross margin in the periods such sales occur, and gross margin was already flat at 64.1% in both 2025 and 2024 despite revenue growth. <sub>[S30] [S40] [S48]</sub>
- Revenue visibility is deteriorating even as growth accelerates: management states that AI-driven demand has increased trial/acceptance-period contracts and product deferred revenue volatility, and that 'near term revenue trends may not be reflective of current demand levels,' despite 28.6% YoY growth. <sub>[S41] [S43] [F46]</sub>
- The AI demand driver is double-edged: large customers have paired AI infrastructure spending with cost-reduction measures and reduced non-AI capex, and management explicitly states the long-term trajectory of AI-driven demand 'remains unknown,' undercutting extrapolation of the 28.8% product revenue growth. <sub>[S31] [S41] [S46]</sub>
- Supply chain cost pressure is intensifying, with management citing 'recent tightening of supply conditions in the memory market' and warning gross margins 'could be further impacted' if tariffs escalate or costs cannot be passed on, a risk sitting directly beneath the 64.1% gross margin and $5.8B gross profit reported. <sub>[S44] [F8] [F41]</sub>
- Capital expenditures nearly quadrupled to $119.5M in FY2025 from $32.0M in FY2024, a much sharper jump than any prior year, raising the question of whether the 'capital-light' characterization will hold as the company scales to meet AI-driven demand commitments. <sub>[F28] [F27] [F25] [F26]</sub>
