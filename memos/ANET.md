## Thesis

Arista's software-enabled networking platform delivered 28.6% YoY revenue growth to $9.0B with 64.1% gross margin, 42.8% operating margin, and 47.2% FCF margin ($4.3B FCF), while ROE reached 28.4% and equity grew to $12.4B — a highly efficient, cash-generative compounder riding AI-driven data center demand. However, the growth and margin profile is less structurally durable than headline metrics suggest: two customers represented 26% and 16% of FY2025 revenue with historically volatile mix (18-21% and 15-21% ranges over the prior two years), gross margin stability is explicitly tied to large-customer sales mix rather than structural insulation, capex more than tripled YoY ($119.5M vs $32.0M) amid tariff and memory-market cost pressure that management says could further hit margins, and management explicitly states AI demand's long-term trajectory is unknown — making the current growth rate difficult to underwrite as durable. <sub>[F46] [F4] [F41] [F42] [F49] [F48] [F45] [F36] [S28] [S39] [S24] [S31] [S48] [F28] [F27] [S44]</sub>

## Bull case

- Revenue grew 28.6% YoY to $9.0B in FY2025, continuing a strong multi-year trajectory from $4.4B in FY2022 with a 3-year CAGR of 27.1%. <sub>[F46] [F4] [F1] [F47]</sub>
- Profitability is high and expanding: 64.1% gross margin, 42.8% operating margin, and 39.0% net margin, with operating income rising from $1.5B in FY2022 to $3.9B in FY2025. <sub>[F41] [F42] [F43] [F9] [F12]</sub>
- Cash conversion is strong: operating cash flow of $4.4B and free cash flow of $4.3B (47.2% FCF margin), both exceeding net income of $3.5B, funding $1.2B (13.7% of revenue) of R&D. <sub>[F24] [F48] [F49] [F16] [F20] [F44]</sub>
- The balance sheet strengthened materially, with stockholders' equity growing from $4.9B in FY2022 to $12.4B in FY2025 and ROE holding at 28.4%, while diluted shares stayed roughly flat (1.28B vs 1.27B in FY2022, -0.4% YoY). <sub>[F33] [F36] [F45] [F40] [F37] [F50]</sub>
- Arista is broadening its addressable market beyond core data-center switching into Enterprise campus and WAN via CloudVision and the newly added VeloCloud SD-WAN portfolio, diversifying revenue mix (Core 65%, Cognitive Adjacencies 18%, Software/Services 17% of FY2025 revenue). <sub>[S11] [S14] [S37]</sub>

## Bear case (after red-team review)

- Customer concentration is extreme and volatile: one end customer represented 26%, 20%, and 18% of total revenue and another 16%, 15%, and 21% for 2025, 2024, and 2023 respectively — any shift in their capex plans could materially move the top line, and management explicitly expects this variability to continue. <sub>[S28] [S39]</sub>
- Management states the long-term trajectory of AI-related demand is 'unknown' and that demand estimates for new products are 'difficult to forecast,' while large customers' AI prioritization has at times coincided with cost-reduction measures in non-AI capex — directly undermining the durability of the 28.6% YoY growth rate. <sub>[S31] [S41] [F46]</sub>
- The 64.1% gross margin is not structurally insulated from concentration risk: management states large-customer sales come with pricing discounts, bundled upgrades, and extended warranties that reduce margins in the periods such sales occur, and gross margin will 'continue to fluctuate' based on this mix. <sub>[S30] [S40] [S48] [F41]</sub>
- The asset-light narrative is undercut by capex more than tripling from $32.0M in FY2024 to $119.5M in FY2025, alongside management warnings that tightening memory-market conditions and potential tariff escalation could further pressure gross margins if costs cannot be passed to customers. <sub>[F27] [F28] [S44]</sub>
- Arista is singularly dependent on Broadcom for merchant silicon; failure of this vendor to innovate, supply sufficient quantities, or continue collaborating could delay product launches, reduce gross margins, or make products less competitive. <sub>[S24] [S27]</sub>
- Cash & equivalents actually declined from $2.8B in FY2024 to $2.0B in FY2025 despite operating cash flow rising to $4.4B, indicating cash is being deployed elsewhere rather than accumulating as a buffer. <sub>[F31] [F32] [F24]</sub>
- Rising customer trials and acceptance-period contracts have increased volatility in product deferred revenue balances, and failure to satisfy trial/contract requirements could force product returns, block revenue recognition, and require inventory write-downs — a revenue-quality risk not captured in headline growth metrics; this compounds with geographic concentration, as Non-Americas revenue reaching 20.9% of total in 2025 was 'primarily driven by changes in the geographic mix of sales to our large global customers.' <sub>[S41] [S46]</sub>

## Diligence questions for a human

1. What specifically drove the tripling of capex from $32.0M to $119.5M — is it discretionary supply-chain investment or a structural shift toward owning more manufacturing/inventory infrastructure?
2. What is the identity and contractual structure (multi-year commitments vs. purchase orders) of the two customers representing 26% and 16% of revenue, and how much visibility does Arista have into their forward capex plans?
3. Does Arista have any qualified second-source or roadmap contingency for merchant silicon if Broadcom's collaboration or supply falters?
4. How much of the flat 64.1% gross margin in 2025 vs 2024 was masked by product/customer mix shifts, and what would margin look like excluding the two largest customers?
5. What is the magnitude and trend of product deferred revenue tied to customer trials/acceptance periods, and how large is exposure to potential product returns or inventory write-downs?
6. What is driving the decline in cash & equivalents despite rising operating cash flow — buybacks, working capital build, or other capital allocation choices?
7. How exposed is the 20.9% Non-Americas revenue mix to tariff/export-control risk, and is it concentrated in the same large customers driving overall concentration risk?
8. What would blended revenue growth look like if the two largest customers' non-AI capex normalizes or declines, per management's own cited risk of cost-reduction measures?
