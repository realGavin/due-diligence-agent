## Thesis

Costco's membership model generates highly durable, recurring revenue—Executive members grew to 38,700 thousand from 32,300 thousand over 2023-2025 and now drive ~73.6% of worldwide net sales with an 89.8% renewal rate—supporting steady top-line growth ($227.0B to $275.2B FY2022-FY2025, 8.2% YoY) and a strong 27.8% ROE on a net-cash balance sheet (-$8.4B net debt). However, the stock's premium valuation (49.54x P/E, 51.19x P/FCF) leaves little cushion given structurally thin margins (3.8% operating, 2.9% net) and rising capex ($3.9B to $5.5B) that compress free cash flow to a 2.8% margin, making the investment case dependent on continued membership penetration and warehouse expansion rather than margin upside. <sub>[S13] [S14] [F1] [F4] [F40] [F39] [F44] [F47] [F48] [F37] [F38] [F17] [F20] [F43]</sub>

## Bull case

- Executive membership—already 73.6% of worldwide net sales—continues to grow (38,700 thousand vs 35,400 thousand and 32,300 thousand in 2024/2023) with an 89.8% worldwide renewal rate (92.3% in U.S./Canada), evidencing sticky recurring fee income. <sub>[S13] [S14]</sub>
- Revenue has grown from $227.0B (FY2022) to $275.2B (FY2025), 8.2% YoY, while operating income rose from $7.8B to $10.4B over the same period, showing durable top-line and earnings momentum. <sub>[F1] [F4] [F40] [F5] [F8]</sub>
- The balance sheet remains net-cash positive (-$8.4B net debt, $14.2B cash vs $5.7B long-term debt), giving flexibility to fund continued warehouse expansion without dilution (diluted shares flat at 444.8M FY2022 vs FY2025, 0.0% YoY change). <sub>[F44] [F24] [F28] [F36] [F33] [F45]</sub>
- Operating cash flow of $13.3B in FY2025 comfortably exceeds net income of $8.1B, underpinning the capacity to self-fund expansion even as capex rises. <sub>[F16] [F12]</sub>

## Bear case (after red-team review)

- The 27.8% ROE headline is partly inflated by a depressed equity base: stockholders' equity fell from $25.1B (FY2023) to $23.6B (FY2024) before recovering to $29.2B (FY2025), so the return metric does not purely reflect a structurally lean capital base as the original thesis implied. <sub>[F30] [F31] [F32] [F39]</sub>
- Capex has risen every year from $3.9B (FY2022) to $4.3B (FY2023) to $4.7B (FY2024) to $5.5B (FY2025), compressing free cash flow to a 2.8% margin and 2.0% FCF yield — thin support for a 51.19x P/FCF multiple that assumes capex intensity won't keep climbing. <sub>[F17] [F18] [F19] [F20] [F43] [F49] [F48]</sub>
- Profitability is concentrated in Executive members (73.6% of worldwide net sales), and the worldwide renewal rate of 89.8% trails the 92.3% U.S./Canada rate, implying softer renewal economics outside core markets that could pressure fee income supporting thin merchandise margins. <sub>[S13] [S14]</sub>
- Gasoline sales (~10% of total net sales in 2025) expose results to fuel-price volatility, and compensation/benefits — the largest expense after cost of merchandise, with ~5% of 341,000 employees unionized — creates wage-inflation and labor-action risk, both bearing on an already-thin 3.8% operating and 2.9% net margin structure. <sub>[S23] [S30] [F37] [F38]</sub>
- Valuation (49.54x P/E, 51.19x P/FCF) leaves little cushion: the market is pricing continued flawless execution on membership growth and warehouse expansion despite structurally low margins and rising capital intensity. <sub>[F47] [F48] [F37] [F38]</sub>
