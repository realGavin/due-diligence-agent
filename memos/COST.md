## Thesis

Costco's membership-driven warehouse model continues to compound: revenue grew from $227.0B in FY2022 to $275.2B in FY2025 (8.2% YoY, 6.6% 3-yr CAGR), operating income rose from $7.8B to $10.4B, and net income from $5.8B to $8.1B, producing 27.8% ROE despite thin 3.8% operating and 2.9% net margins inherent to the model. Cash conversion is strong ($13.3B operating cash flow vs. $8.1B net income, $7.8B free cash flow) and the balance sheet is conservative (-$8.4B net debt, flat diluted shares at 444.8M), but two structural vulnerabilities deserve elevated weight rather than footnote status: (1) earnings quality is heavily concentrated in Executive members, who already drive approximately 73.6% of worldwide net sales and whose 2%-reward incentive structure ties future net sales and membership-fee growth to a single cohort's continued upgrade and spending behavior, and (2) the company's already-thin 3.8% operating margin leaves little room to absorb wage inflation or labor disruption given that compensation and benefits is explicitly its largest expense after merchandise cost across 341,000 employees (~5% unionized). Additional secondary risks include capex outpacing cash generation (capex from $3.9B to $5.5B against a 2.8% FCF margin), a working-capital-dependent model reliant on selling inventory before paying suppliers, softer international renewal rates (89.8% worldwide vs. 92.3% U.S./Canada) as the company expands into more of these markets (914 warehouses, up from 861), and a gasoline business (~10% of net sales) whose price volatility can distort reported revenue growth. <sub>[F1] [F4] [F40] [F41] [F5] [F8] [F9] [F12] [F39] [F37] [F38] [F16] [F42] [F44] [F36] [S28] [S30] [F17] [F20] [F43] [S18] [S29] [S1] [S23]</sub>

## Bear case (after red-team review)

- Earnings quality is increasingly concentrated in a single, incentive-driven cohort: Executive members, who earn a 2% reward capped at $1,250/year, already account for approximately 73.6% of worldwide net sales, up from a base of 32,300 thousand to 38,700 thousand members, making both net sales and membership-fee income disproportionately exposed to any slowdown in this group's spending or renewal behavior. <sub>[S28]</sub>
- The operating margin buffer is thin relative to labor exposure: compensation and benefits is explicitly Costco's largest expense after merchandise cost, covering 341,000 employees (~5% unionized), while operating margin is only 3.8%, leaving little room to absorb wage inflation or labor disruption without material margin compression. <sub>[S30] [F37]</sub>
- Free cash flow generation is being outpaced by rising capital intensity: capex climbed from $3.9B (FY2022) to $5.5B (FY2025), and despite 8.2% revenue growth, FCF margin was just 2.8% ($7.8B FCF), indicating warehouse expansion is consuming a growing share of operating cash flow. <sub>[F17] [F20] [F40] [F42] [F43]</sub>
- The low-margin model is structurally dependent on favorable working-capital timing: Costco explicitly states it 'often sells inventory before we are required to pay for it, even while taking advantage of early payment discounts,' a dynamic that could be disrupted if supplier payment terms tighten or turnover slows. <sub>[S18]</sub>
- International renewal rates are meaningfully weaker than the U.S./Canada core (89.8% worldwide vs. 92.3% U.S./Canada), and as the company expands its international footprint (914 warehouses, up from 861 two years prior, across 14 countries/territories), this weaker cohort could pressure the blended renewal rate and membership-fee income further. <sub>[S29] [S1]</sub>
- Gasoline, a low-margin and price-volatile category, represented approximately 10% of total net sales in FY2025; swings in fuel prices could inflate or deflate the reported 8.2% YoY revenue growth without a proportional profit contribution, complicating assessment of core warehouse strength. <sub>[S23] [F40]</sub>

## Diligence questions for a human

1. What is the spending trajectory and per-capita net sales of Executive members versus non-Executive members over the last several years, and what would a plateau in Executive upgrade rates imply for both membership fee revenue and net sales growth?
2. How does comparable/core merchandise sales growth look when gasoline and other commodity-price-sensitive categories are stripped out of the 8.2% YoY revenue growth figure?
3. What are the terms and timing of major union contracts, and what wage escalation assumptions are embedded in near-term labor cost planning given the 3.8% operating margin?
4. What is the renewal rate trend by individual country or region (rather than just the worldwide 89.8% blended figure), and which specific international markets are dragging it down?
5. What returns (e.g., payback period, warehouse-level sales ramp) is Costco generating on the incremental capex used for international expansion, particularly in markets with weaker renewal rates?
6. How have days payable outstanding and supplier payment terms trended recently, and is there any indication that early payment discount economics or supplier terms are tightening?
7. What percentage of total capex is allocated to new warehouse openings versus maintenance/remodels, and how does that mix affect the FCF margin trajectory going forward?
