## Thesis

Nike's brand-reset has stabilized reported revenue but not underlying momentum or cash quality: FY2026 revenue of $46.4B vs $46.3B in FY2025 is essentially flat on a reported basis but down 2% on a currency-neutral basis, with Greater China, Converse and EMEA each subtracting further from growth (~2, 1 and 1 percentage points respectively), meaning the 'flat' headline partly reflects currency tailwinds rather than genuine stabilization. Profitability and cash generation have deteriorated faster than revenue: net income fell from $5.7B (FY2024) to $3.1B (FY2026), ROIC declined from 20.2% to 18.7%, and operating cash flow of $2.9B is now below net income of $3.1B for the first time in the multi-year trend (versus $7.4B OCF vs $5.7B net income in FY2024) - a reversal in cash-conversion quality that the balance sheet cannot fully offset. NIKE Direct's 12% Digital decline is not a clean 'deliberate' repositioning but is explicitly attributed by management to reduced traffic, alongside a broader 6%/8% (reported/currency-neutral) NIKE Direct decline and a 4% comp-store drop. On the positive side, the tariff overhang looks largely resolved (of $684M in outstanding IEEPA receivables, Nike had already received $302M and subsequently received substantially all the remainder, alongside a $986M cost-of-sales benefit), and the balance sheet remains conservative (net debt of just $379M against $7.6B cash), giving Nike room to fund an admittedly open-ended restructuring ($385M in FY2026 severance, with management flagging potential additional charges) while Greater China and Converse drag continues through FY2027. <sub>[F4] [F3] [S24] [F12] [F10] [S35] [F16] [F14] [S27] [S40] [F44] [F24] [S39] [S38]</sub>

## Bull case

- The balance sheet remains conservative and gives Nike flexibility to absorb restructuring costs: net debt is just $379M against $7.6B cash and $7.9B long-term debt, and diluted shares continue to shrink (1.48B FY2026 vs 1.49B FY2025), indicating no dilution pressure. <sub>[F44] [F24] [F28] [F45] [F36] [F35]</sub>
- Gross margin actually improved slightly to 42.9% in FY2026 from 42.7% in FY2025, driven by lower warehousing/logistics costs and favorable FX, suggesting some cost discipline is taking hold even as revenue struggles. <sub>[S28]</sub>
- The IEEPA tariff overhang appears largely resolved rather than an open risk: Nike already recognized a $986M cost-of-sales benefit, received $302M of the $684M receivable by fiscal year-end, and subsequently received substantially all of the remainder. <sub>[S40]</sub>
- North America is showing genuine progress against the brand-reset playbook, increasing NIKE, Inc. revenues by ~2 percentage points and driving wholesale revenue growth to $27.5B from $25.9B, while apparel revenue also grew to $13.4B from $13.0B. <sub>[S24] [S26]</sub>

## Bear case (after red-team review)

- Cash-conversion quality has reversed: FY2026 operating cash flow of $2.9B and free cash flow of $2.2B both now sit below net income of $3.1B, a stark change from FY2024 when OCF of $7.4B comfortably exceeded net income of $5.7B, undermining the thesis's reliance on balance-sheet flexibility. <sub>[F16] [F12] [F42] [F14] [F10]</sub>
- Reported revenue flatness ($46.4B vs $46.3B) masks a currency-neutral decline of 2%, with Greater China, Converse and EMEA collectively dragging results down, and management explicitly expects Greater China and Converse headwinds (declining traffic, elevated promotions, higher inventory) to persist throughout fiscal 2027. <sub>[S24] [F4] [F3] [S38]</sub>
- The 12% Digital sales decline is not a controlled strategic repositioning but is attributed by the filing to 'reduced traffic,' part of a broader NIKE Direct deterioration (-6% reported, -8% currency-neutral, comp-store sales -4%) that took Direct revenue from $18.8B to $17.7B, raising doubt about whether the wholesale-shift narrative is intentional or reactive. <sub>[S27]</sub>
- Capital efficiency is deteriorating on a cleaner basis than the ROE headline suggests: ROIC fell from 20.2% to 18.7%, even as ROE of 20.9% is partly inflated by a smaller equity base ($14.9B FY2026) relative to prior periods, so the 'strong ROE' framing overstates underlying profitability leverage. <sub>[S35] [F39] [F32] [F30]</sub>
- Restructuring costs are open-ended: the $385M FY2026 severance charge is not a bounded, one-time item, as management explicitly states it 'may take additional actions which could lead to additional charges in future quarters,' meaning the true cost of the brand reset is unknown. <sub>[S39]</sub>
- Supply-chain risk is structural, not merely tariff-related: certain contract manufacturers are 'highly specialized' and could permanently exit the business if demand shifts, and Nike 'may not be able to locate replacement facilities... in a timely manner or at all,' a potentially irreversible concentration risk. <sub>[S2]</sub>
