## Thesis

NVIDIA has scaled from a GPU/gaming company into a data center scale AI infrastructure company, with revenue growing from $27.0B in FY2023 to $215.9B in FY2026 (65.5% YoY, 100.0% 3-year CAGR) and industry-leading margins and cash generation. However, the FY2026 results also show early signs of margin quality erosion (Hopper-to-Blackwell transition costs, a $4.5B H20 charge, and a worsening inventory-provision drag), a largely closed China market under export controls, and a growing web of circular customer financing and contingent infrastructure guarantees that complicate a clean 'platform moat' narrative. The core franchise (CUDA ecosystem, full-stack co-design, networking lock-in) remains intact, but the thesis must now weigh demand-side bottlenecks and disclosure-flagged risks alongside the growth and profitability data. <sub>[S1] [F1] [F4] [F50] [F51] [S26] [S45] [S30] [S19] [S38] [S22] [S41]</sub>

## Bull case

- Revenue grew from $27.0B (FY2023) to $215.9B (FY2026), a 65.5% YoY increase and 100.0% 3-year CAGR, with gross margin at 71.1%, operating margin 60.4%, and net margin 55.6% in FY2026. <sub>[F1] [F4] [F50] [F51] [F45] [F46] [F47]</sub>
- Cash generation is exceptional and closely tracks earnings: operating cash flow reached $102.7B versus net income of $120.1B, with free cash flow of $96.7B (44.8% FCF margin) in FY2026, up from operating cash flow of just $5.6B in FY2023. <sub>[F24] [F16] [F52] [F53] [F21]</sub>
- The balance sheet is conservatively levered with net debt of -$2.1B (long-term debt $8.5B vs. cash $10.6B), and stockholders' equity grew from $22.1B (FY2023) to $157.3B (FY2026), supporting a 76.3% return on equity. <sub>[F54] [F36] [F32] [F37] [F40] [F49]</sub>
- The durable moat rests on the CUDA software platform and full-stack co-design (hardware, networking, systems, software, algorithms), exemplified by Blackwell's ability to interconnect hundreds of thousands of GPUs as a single computer, reinforced by cumulative R&D investment of over $76.7 billion since inception and NVLink Fusion extending the ecosystem into hyperscaler custom silicon. <sub>[S1] [S2] [S4] [S5] [S13]</sub>
- Shareholder dilution is not a concern: diluted share count declined from 25.07B (FY2023) to 24.51B (FY2026), a -1.2% YoY change in the most recent year. <sub>[F41] [F44] [F55]</sub>

## Bear case (after red-team review)

- FY2026 gross margin compression was not simple scaling but was driven by the Hopper-to-Blackwell product transition and a $4.5 billion H20 charge, with the unfavorable inventory-provision impact on gross margin worsening from 2.3% in FY2025 to 2.6% in FY2026 — a deteriorating trend in inventory risk exposure that undercuts a pure 'operating leverage' narrative. <sub>[S26] [S45] [S30]</sub>
- The China AI chip market has been largely closed off: the H20 license requirement drove a $4.5 billion charge, licensed H20 revenue was only about $60 million, and the new H200 pathway has generated zero revenue to date, carries a 25% U.S. import tariff, and has no certainty of Chinese import approval — a significant, unresolved demand overhang. <sub>[S19] [S38] [S20] [S39]</sub>
- Revenue growth quality is compromised by circular financing risk: NVIDIA invested $17.5 billion in private companies and infrastructure funds, primarily early-stage AI startups that are themselves NVIDIA customers, many of which may never become profitable, raising the possibility that some reported demand is indirectly self-funded rather than organic. <sub>[S22] [S41]</sub>
- NVIDIA has taken on $3.5 billion in land, power, and shell guarantees to early-stage datacenter partners; if partner operations and escrow prove insufficient, NVIDIA may have to assume the underlying leases itself, converting counterparty risk into direct real-estate/infrastructure liability not reflected in headline margin and cash flow metrics. <sub>[S23] [S42]</sub>
- Continued growth depends on customers' and partners' ability to build out data centers, but the filing explicitly flags data center, energy, and capital availability as binding constraints, with multi-year energy capacity expansion facing significant regulatory, technical, and construction challenges that could delay deployments and cap AI adoption — a demand-side risk outside NVIDIA's control. <sub>[S17] [S36]</sub>
- The headline 100.0% 3-year revenue CAGR overstates current momentum since the most recent year's growth of 65.5% YoY is well below that average, and management guides to a near-term Gaming headwind from supply constraints in Q1 FY2027 despite 41% YoY Gaming growth in FY2026, suggesting the hyper-growth trajectory may not extrapolate cleanly. <sub>[F50] [F51] [S25] [S44]</sub>
- Despite being framed as an asset-light platform business, capital expenditures have accelerated sharply from $1.1B (FY2024) to $3.2B (FY2025) to $6.0B (FY2026), indicating rising capital intensity. <sub>[F26] [F27] [F28]</sub>

## Diligence questions for a human

1. What is the trajectory of H20/H200 China revenue over the next 2-3 quarters, and does management have a credible plan if Chinese import approval is denied entirely?
2. How much of NVIDIA's reported Data Center revenue growth is attributable to customers that also received NVIDIA equity or debt investment, and how correlated is investment timing with subsequent purchase orders?
3. What are the specific terms, expiration, and expected loss-given-default assumptions on the $3.5 billion land/power/shell guarantees, and how large could this contingent exposure grow?
4. What is the expected useful life and obsolescence risk of Blackwell-generation inventory given the one-year product cadence and history of write-downs (H20 charge, rising inventory-provision drag)?
5. How dependent is FY2027 guidance on continued hyperscaler capex growth, and what specific data center/energy capacity constraints have customers flagged as limiting near-term deployment?
6. What percentage of Data Center revenue is concentrated among the top 5-10 customers, and how much of that is early-stage/non-investment-grade counterparties versus established hyperscalers?
7. Is the rising capex trend (from $1.1B to $3.2B to $6.0B) a precursor to NVIDIA building more of its own infrastructure/manufacturing capacity, and what would that mean for the 'asset-light' characterization going forward?
8. How does management define and quantify 'excess inventory and purchase obligations' risk for the next generation (Rubin) product transition, given the H20/Blackwell precedent?
