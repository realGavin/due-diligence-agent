## Thesis

NVIDIA's platform strategy (GPUs, CUDA, networking, full-stack software) has driven revenue from $27.0B in FY2023 to $215.9B in FY2026 with 71.1% gross margin, 60.4% operating margin, and a net-cash balance sheet (-$2.1B net debt), backed by $102.7B of operating cash flow and a 76.3% return on equity. However, growth is decelerating (100.0% 3-year CAGR vs. 65.5% latest-year growth), the China AI chip market remains largely closed (only ~$60 million in H20 revenue after a $4.5 billion charge, with H200 re-entry subject to a 25% tariff and no revenue yet), and the stock already prices in continued hypergrowth at a 45.42x P/E and 1.8% FCF yield, leaving little room for error from product-cadence delays or demand normalization. <sub>[F1] [F4] [F45] [F46] [F54] [F24] [F49] [F50] [F51] [S19] [S38] [S20] [S39] [F57] [F59]</sub>

**PM's rationale:** The core financial engine remains extraordinary — 65.5% growth, 71.1% gross margin, $102.7B operating cash flow, net-cash balance sheet — but the red team surfaced two objections material enough to warrant caution beyond the original demand-normalization thesis: (1) the $17.5B investment in AI model makers that also purchase NVIDIA's products raises a legitimate circularity question about how much of the reported hypergrowth reflects organic end-demand versus NVIDIA-funded demand, and (2) the $3.5B in datacenter partner guarantees represent contingent liabilities not visible in the -$2.1B net debt figure, meaning the balance-sheet strength claim is somewhat overstated. ## Diligence questions

**Q: What portion of NVIDIA's reported Data Center revenue growth is attributable to purchases by companies in which NVIDIA holds equity stakes (the $17.5B investment portfolio), and how much of that revenue would be considered circular financing versus independent end-demand?**

- During the last fiscal year, Nvidia invested $17.5 billion in private companies and infrastructure funds, "primarily to support early-stage startups," according to its annual filing with the SEC. <sub>[W4]</sub>
- The company said those investments include AI model companies that purchase its products directly or through cloud service providers. <sub>[W5]</sub>
- Nvidia held $99 billion in equity investments as of July 26, including roughly $48 billion in publicly traded stocks and other marketable securities <sub>[W6]</sub>
- CFO Colette Kress addressed "circular financing" criticism directly on the earnings call, including Nvidia's new partnership for an OpenAI data center in Ohio, saying "We recognize the scale of this support, and we know some will call this circular financing. We see it differently," describing them as investments in "once-in-a-generation companies." <sub>[W7]</sub>
- NVIDIA disclosed $99 billion in equity stakes, but the other side of that money is not required to be disclosed <sub>[W9]</sub>

**Q: What is the current book value and expected utilization/default risk profile of the $3.5B in land, power, and shell guarantees to early-stage datacenter partners, and how many partners are showing financial distress?**

- NVIDIA entered into land, power, and shell guarantees for select AI cloud partners' data center lease obligations in the event of their default, with maximum gross exposure under all agreements of $3.5 billion. <sub>[W52]</sub>
- The guarantee is classified as a credit derivative with changes in fair value recognized in Other income and expense, and has an insignificant fair value. <sub>[W53]</sub>
- Nvidia's Q1 FY2027 10-Q caps maximum gross exposure across all of its partner facility lease guarantees at $3.5 billion, shrinking as partners pay their lessors, with $712 million sitting in escrow against it. <sub>[W54]</sub>
- NVIDIA states it provided $3.5 billion in land, power, and shell guarantees to early‑stage companies, generally over multi‑year periods, and if the escrow and the partners' operating activities are not sufficient to cover an event of default under these guarantees, it may elect to assume the underlying leases for internal use or sublease them to third parties. <sub>[W55]</sub>
- The first one, disclosed in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, capped at $860 million with $470 million of escrow behind it, where the partner separately contracted to sell the data center cloud capacity, and Nvidia retained the option to assume the lease for internal use or sublease it if the escrow and that contract came up short <sub>[W56]</sub>

**Q: Is there any indication of actual volume or dollar commitments under the new H200 China export license beyond the initial approval, and what is the realistic timeline and total addressable revenue given the 25% tariff and inspection requirements?**

- A handful of Chinese companies have been approved by the U.S. Commerce Department to purchase H200s, including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and JD.com <sub>[W13]</sub>
- Shifted export license review for NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X chips from presumption of denial to case-by-case review for China—subject to a 25% tariff, 50% volume cap, third-party testing, and strict KYC requirements <sub>[W14]</sub>
- Trump signed a proclamation entailing a 25% tariff on advanced AI semiconductors produced outside the U.S. and then passed through the U.S. before export <sub>[W15]</sub>
- "We're allowing them to do it, but the United States is getting 25% of the chips, in terms of the dollar value." <sub>[W16]</sub>
- The company's Q1 2027 revenue forecast doesn't account for a single cent of Chinese datacenter revenues <sub>[W17]</sub>
- "While small amounts of H200 products for China based customers were approved by the US government, we have yet to generate any revenue." <sub>[W18]</sub>
- It's unclear exactly how many H200 chips were sold to Chinese buyers in the second quarter, but the count fell below the US limit due to blocks imposed by the Chinese government <sub>[W19]</sub>
- Halted production of China-configured H200 units in March 2026 and moved the freed TSMC capacity to Vera Rubin, judging near-term China revenue too uncertain to plan around, though it said it could restart or expand H200 output within roughly three months if conditions shift <sub>[W20]</sub>
- "the Chinese market could be worth $50 billion per year" <sub>[W21]</sub>
- Approximately 20-25% of Nvidia's data center revenue, translating to roughly $10-15 billion annually at FY2025 run rates <sub>[W22]</sub>
- The number that matters now isn't a Washington ceiling—it's a Beijing decision to keep the bulk of its own approved quota sitting unused <sub>[W23]</sub>
- Strategically delaying import clearances for Nvidia products since late 2025 <sub>[W24]</sub>

**Q: How much of hyperscaler capex growth (the primary source of NVIDIA Data Center demand) is funded by debt or off-balance-sheet vehicles versus free cash flow, and are there signs of deceleration in hyperscaler capex guidance for the next fiscal year?**

- Incremental annual debt rose from 9% of capex in FY24 to 32% LTM by mid-2026, and equity has now returned to the funding mix: Alphabet priced an $84.75bn equity raise in June 2026, and Oracle plans $40bn of combined debt and equity for FY27 <sub>[W57]</sub>
- Surging AI capex and declining free cash flows (AI costs are front-loaded with returns expected over a longer horizon) are the main drivers of the funding shift. <sub>[W58]</sub>
- Aggregate lease-related commitments, not recognized as balance sheet liabilities, are material across the five hyperscalers at ~$820bn <sub>[W59]</sub>
- A few are getting creative with how they are structuring these debt issuances as well, with Meta using an off-balance-sheet special purpose vehicle to fund a data center in Louisiana <sub>[W60]</sub>
- Meta issued a record-breaking $30 billion in bonds, Alphabet raised $25 billion, Oracle $18 billion, and Amazon $15 billion — this group of companies had already issued over $100 billion in U.S. bonds as of March 2026, nearly their total for all of 2025 <sub>[W61] [W62]</sub>
- Alphabet turned free cash flow negative in Q2 2026 for the first time, while its long-term debt more than doubled to $98 billion over the first half of the year, and Amazon's long-term debt jumped 81% to $119 billion in the first quarter alone <sub>[W63]</sub>
- Hood guided full-year operating margins down less than 1 point and said Microsoft expects to remain free cash flow positive in FY27 <sub>[W66]</sub>
- Amazon raised its 2026 number to ~$220B, Google raised to $195-205B, Meta lifted the floor of its guide to $130-145B <sub>[W67]</sub>
- Meta raised 2026 capex guidance to $125–$145 billion from $115–$135 billion, explicitly citing memory pricing <sub>[W68]</sub>
- Based on current guidance of $732.5 billion, Big Tech capex would need to rise by 36.5% YoY to hit $1 trillion next year, a stark deceleration from 2026's guided 79% growth, needing to add just $267.5 billion versus the $332.5 billion increase guided for 2026 <sub>[W69]</sub>
- The largest component of capex has shifted toward shorter-lived assets like CPUs and GPUs — "If the demand environment changes, you just slow down what is, in fact, the largest component." <sub>[W71]</sub>

**Q: What is the expected timing and yield/output improvement of the Rubin platform launch, and are there any early signs of supply chain or production delays similar to past Blackwell ramp issues?**

- In fiscal year 2026, NVIDIA unveiled the Rubin platform, which is expected to commence production shipments in the second half of fiscal year 2027 <sub>[W25]</sub>
- Launched the Rubin platform with a stack of new chips, and the AI systems will be available in the second half of 2026 <sub>[W26]</sub>
- Production hardware delivered to partners in time for them to offer products and services in the second half of 2026 <sub>[W27]</sub>
- "We are on track to deliver Vera Rubin about Q3 time frame of next year, continuing our once-a-year cycle." <sub>[W28]</sub>
- Up to a 10x reduction in cost per token compared to Blackwell <sub>[W29]</sub>
- 1.7M to 1.8M Rubin units for 2026 <sub>[W31]</sub>
- Nvidia's next-gen Rubin GPUs may end up shipping later and in smaller volumes than anticipated due to supply chain challenges, with Rubin now expected to account for 22 percent of Nvidia's high-end GPU shipments in 2026, down from a previous forecast of 29 percent <sub>[W32]</sub>
- The time required to validate the newer HBM4 memory used by the chips, challenges with the migration to Nvidia's faster ConnectX-9 NICs, the system's higher overall power consumption, and the more advanced liquid cooling requirements <sub>[W33]</sub>
- Struggling with delays from suppliers like SK Hynix and Samsung for their HBM4 high-bandwidth memory product <sub>[W34]</sub>
- The ongoing memory shortage having pushed major suppliers' lead times out beyond 2028 <sub>[W35]</sub>
- Taping out of the design had occurred last August <sub>[W36]</sub>
- A thermal lid manufacturing challenge affecting Rubin GPU production — the metal cover that sits on top of the GPU die to dissipate heat, where manufacturing tolerances are tight and defects at scale can stall assembly lines <sub>[W37]</sub>
- Rubin is expected to begin ramping in July, though initial volume will be lower than originally projected <sub>[W38]</sub>
- Nvidia continued to struggle with Blackwell production after launch — due to the complexity of the chip, fewer suitable GPUs initially rolled off TSMC's production line than expected, though eventually all issues were ironed out and Blackwell chips are now leaving factories in high volumes <sub>[W39]</sub>
- Blackwell series shipments are projected to grow markedly from 61% to 71% of NVIDIA's high-end GPU shipments in 2026 <sub>[W40]</sub>

**Q: To what extent are open-source foundation models (e.g., those deployed on competitor silicon) gaining adoption among NVIDIA's largest AI model-maker customers, and is there measurable market share erosion in inference workloads?**

- Running GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 inference on MI300X through ONNX Runtime with no CUDA dependency <sub>[W41]</sub>
- Anthropic and AMD outlined a strategic partnership to deploy up to 2 gigawatts of AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs in AMD Helios rackscale solutions, with a multiyear engineering collaboration to use Claude to accelerate AMD software development <sub>[W42]</sub>
- OpenAI is optimizing GPT-class workloads on AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs and AMD Helios racks using Triton and ROCm <sub>[W43]</sub>
- A first-class citizen in PyTorch, vLLM and SGLang, even as ROCm's training story trails its inference story <sub>[W44] [W45]</sub>
- Building frontier inference clusters that run DeepSeek, Qwen, Mamba and Llama <sub>[W46]</sub>
- An estimated 80-85 percent of data center AI accelerator market by revenue in 2026, down from approximately 92 percent in 2023 <sub>[W47]</sub>
- Exceeding 90%, while inference is 60-75% due to custom silicon and CPU competition <sub>[W48]</sub>
- Custom ASICs from Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta growing at 44.6% CAGR, targeting inference workloads that now represent two-thirds of all AI compute <sub>[W49]</sub>
- Jensen Huang stated "we are growing share in inference, and we're growing share in inference very, very quickly," <sub>[W50]</sub>
- "Nvidia is the only platform that runs every frontier AI model," naming Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, Meta and Google's Gemini <sub>[W51]</sub>
